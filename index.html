About the course

About Microbiology

Microbiology is the study of the bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa and helminths that shape human health. In BIOL 442: General Microbiology and Public Health, students examine how these organisms are built, how they live and reproduce, how they cause disease, and how we identify, treat and prevent infection, from individual patients to global epidemics and pandemics.

The course is taught as preparation for careers in healthcare. Most students are headed toward nursing and other health professions, and the lab is where much of the learning happens: microscopy, culturing bacteria, and identifying unknown microorganisms from a simulated patient infection.

The organisms of microbiology

The five groups studied in BIOL 442, with their typical sizes.

Fig. 1

Bacteria

e.g. Escherichia, Staphylococcus

0.5–5 µm

Single cells without a nucleus. Most are harmless or essential; a few cause disease.

Fig. 2

Viruses

e.g. influenza, SARS-CoV-2

20–300 nm

Not cells: genetic material in a protein coat that must take over a host cell to replicate.

Fig. 3

Fungi

e.g. Candida, Aspergillus

Yeast cells 3–10 µm

Yeasts and molds. Some cause infections; others give us bread and penicillin.

Fig. 4

Protozoa

e.g. Plasmodium, Giardia

10–200 µm

Single cells with a nucleus that move and feed, including the parasite that causes malaria.

Fig. 5

Helminths

e.g. tapeworms, roundworms

Millimeters to meters

Parasitic worms: multicellular animals, often visible without a microscope.

Student learning outcomes

What students learn

By the end of BIOL 442, students can:

  1. Apply microbiology concepts to current issues in human health, biotechnology, infectious disease and the environment.
  2. Analyze how the human body interacts with microorganisms through symbiotic relationships and defense mechanisms.
  3. Evaluate the role microbes play in epidemiology, and the methods used to control the spread of disease.
  4. Conduct experiments involving culture and transfer of microbes, microscopy, biochemical tests, DNA techniques and diagnostic media.
  5. Evaluate infectious diseases: causative agents, signs and symptoms, pathogenesis, virulence, epidemiology, diagnosis, treatment and prevention.

Requirements & Materials

Prerequisite

CHEM 305 (Introduction to Chemistry) or CHEM 400 (General Chemistry I) with a grade of C or better.

Equivalent chemistry courses from other colleges may qualify. Bring official or unofficial transcripts to the first day of class.

Recommended first

Some biology coursework helps: BIOL C1000 (Introduction to Biology with Lab), BIOL 310 (General Biology), or ENGWR 110 (College Reading and Writing Skills).

Textbook & Lab Manual

Microbiology Fundamentals: A Clinical Approach, 4th ed. (Cowan & Smith), with the companion lab manual, 4th ed. (Obenauf & Finazzo).
